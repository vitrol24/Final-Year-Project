Building A Model

After creating a project, click on the "Open Editor" button. The editor will be displayed as shown below.

Blocks

After opening the editor, you will see in the left panel that there are five blocks and the first one is selected. Blocks are simply the rooms of your building. Move your mouse onto the grid, hold down the left mouse button, drag, and release to create the block. As shown below. Try out the other four blocks as well.

Now you will notice a new button on the right hand side, under the blocks table. This represents your new block. Click on it to open the blocks editor as shown below.

In the blocks editor are the following fields:

  • Type - This is a read only field showing the type of object being edited.
  • Name - This is the name of this block.
  • Visible - Turning this on/off will show/hide the block. It doesn't delete it.
  • Walls
    • East Wall- Turning this on/off will add/remove the wall on the eastern side of the block. It isn't hidden, but completely removed.
    • West Wall - Turning this on/off will add/remove the wall on the western side of the block. It isn't hidden, but completely removed.
    • North Wall - Turning this on/off will add/remove the wall on the northern side of the block. It isn't hidden, but completely removed.
    • South Wall - Turning this on/off will add/remove the wall on the southern side of the block. It isn't hidden, but completely removed.
  • Floor Material - The materials the floor is made of. There are 5 layers in total. It must have atleast one layer.
    • Layer 1 - The top most layer of the floor.
    • Layer 2 - The layer right below layer 1.
    • Layer 3 - The layer right below layer 2.
    • Layer 4 - The layer right below layer 3.
    • Layer 5 - The bottom most layer of the floor.
  • Exteror Walls Material - The exterior materials of the wall. There are 5 layers in total. It must have atleast one layer.
    • Layer 1 - The outer most layer that comes into contact with the outside environment.
    • Layer 2 - The layer right below layer 1.
    • Layer 3 - The layer right below layer 2.
    • Layer 4 - The layer right below layer 3.
    • Layer 5 - The layer right below layer 4.
  • Interior Walls Material - The interior materials of the wall. There are 5 layers in total. It must have atleast one layer.
    • Layer 1 - The inner most layer that comes into contact with the inside of the house.
    • Layer 2 - The layer right below layer 1.
    • Layer 3 - The layer right below layer 2.
    • Layer 4 - The layer right below layer 3.
    • Layer 5 - The layer right below layer 4.
  • Roof Material - The materials the roof is made of. There are 5 layers in total. It must have atleast one layer.
    • Layer 1 - The top most layer of the roof.
    • Layer 2 - The layer right below layer 1.
    • Layer 3 - The layer right below layer 2.
    • Layer 4 - The layer right below layer 3.
    • Layer 5 - The layer right below layer 4.
  • Delete Block - This button deletes the block from the floor plan.

Sustainable Contruction Materials

Zambian Climatic Context: Characterized by a tropical climate with three distinct seasons: a cool, dry winter (May-August), a hot, dry spring (September–October), and a warm, wet summer (November–April). Key architectural objectives are maximizing thermal mass to buffer diurnal temperature swings, controlling solar heat gain, and managing high humidity/rainfall during the wet season.

1. Wall Materials & Multi-Layer Assemblages

Walls constitute the primary thermal envelope. In Zambia, balancing thermal mass (to delay heat transfer during hot days) and insulation is vital. Below are the optimal configurations using the specified materials.

This configuration maximizes thermal delay and insulation efficiency, perfectly suited for the hot dry season before the rains.

Layer Material Reasoning
Layer 1 (External) Red Clay Face Brick (115mm) High durability, low maintenance, excellent thermal mass.
Layer 2 Grey Cement Plaster (15mm) Structural backing and moisture barrier.
Layer 3 (Core) Glass Wool (50mm) or EPS Thermal insulation block against ambient heat.
Layer 4 Cement Block or Clay Brick (150mm) Internal load-bearing thermal mass.
Layer 5 (Internal) White Plaster or Gypsum Plaster (12mm) High albedo internal finish.

Material Suitability Matrix for Walls

Material Performance Rating Zambian Climate Guidelines and Best Practices
Red Clay Face Brick Excellent Highly sustainable due to local soil availability. Excellent thermal mass property slows down heat transfer into interiors during peak daylight (12:00 - 15:00)
Concrete Blocks / Cement Block Moderate Widely available but has lower thermal resistance than clay. Best utilized for internal structural partitions where they are shielded from direct solar radiation.
Glass Wool / EPS / Polyurethane Foam Excellent ( Core ) Crucial for cavity walls. Prevents heat radiating from the outer brick layer from penetrating the inner living space. Glass wool is preferred for fire safety.
White Plaster / Gypsum Plaster Recommended White internal finishes improve natural daylighting. Gypsum plaster provides a smooth finish and acts as a minor humidity buffer during rainy seasons.
Aluminum Cladding Conditional Can reflect solar radiation but risks creating a heat-trap if installed without a ventilated air cavity beneath it. High embodied energy.

2. Roof Materials & Thermal Performance

The roof receives the highest solar radiation in Zambia. Mitigation of radiant heat transfer into the ceiling void is a critical priority.

Optimal Roof Insulation Assembly

Layer Material Reasoning
Layer 1 (Top) Concrete Roof Tiles or Steel Roofing Tiles Heavy mass or high reflectance.
Layer 2 Air Cavity (Ventilated Attic Space) Dissipates accumulated radiant heat.
Layer 3 Polyurethane Foam or Glass Wool Layer Retards heat downward conduction.
Layer 4 (Ceiling) Plasterboard (Drywall) (12mm) Smooth internal boundary finish.

Guidelines by Material Type

  • Concrete Roof Tiles: Excellent choice for residential structures. Their high thermal mass retards solar heat penetration, releasing stored heat during the cooler night hours.
  • Steel Roofing Tiles / Aluminum Roofing Sheets: Lightweight and highly reflective if coated properly. Aluminum offers superb corrosion resistance during Zambia’s heavy wet season. They must be coupled with under-roof insulation (e.g., PU Foam) to avoid massive radiant heat transfers and acoustic noise during heavy downpours.
  • Corrugated Iron Sheets: Least sustainable option thermally. They transfer heat instantly into the building envelope. If used due to budget constraints, a minimum 50mm Glass Wool or EPS layer must be installed on top of the drywall ceiling.

3. Floor Materials & Thermal Mass Optimization

Floors in passive solar design act as a thermal sink. During Zambia's cool dry season, floors can store heat during the day and radiate it at night. In hot seasons, they remain comfortably cool if shaded from direct sunlight.

Optimal High-Mass Floor Assembly

Layer Material Reasoning
Layer 1 (Surface) Granite, Marble, or Polished Concrete Ultimate thermal sinks.
Layer 2 Screed / Mortar Bed Mechanical bond layer.
Layer 3 Reinforced Concrete Slab Primary load bearing and structural mass.
Layer 4 Hardcore / Compacted Earth Direct thermal connection to the ground.

Best Practices for Floor Selection

  • Granite, Marble, & Polished Concrete: Top tier choices for living areas. They feel cool to the touch because they draw heat away from the human body rapidly, drastically reducing the localized cooling load requirements.
  • Ceramic & Porcelain Tiles: Highly sustainable, cost-effective, and practical for wet areas. They maintain good thermal contact with the slab, allowing the floor to function effectively as a passive heat sink.
  • Hardwood Flooring: Insulates the floor slab. While visually warm and luxurious, it reduces the floor’s capacity to act as a cooling thermal mass during hot months. Best limited to bedrooms where nighttime warmth is desired.
  • PVC/Vinyl Tiles: Low thermal capacity and high synthetic content. Not recommended for living spaces aiming for optimal passive climatic control.

4. Window Glazing & Solar Heat Gain Control

Windows are responsible for the largest percentage of structural heat gain. In Zambia, controlling the Solar Heat Gain Coefficient (SHGC) is mandatory to prevent greenhouse effects inside buildings.

Glazing Type Thermal Efficiency Zambian Application Strategy
Double Glazing Low-e 4-6-4mm air Premium Standard The absolute best choice for North and West-facing facades. The Low-E (low-emissivity) coating reflects long-wave infrared heat while letting in visible light.
Double Glazing Clear 4-6-4mm air Good Provides great acoustic insulation and controls conductive heat, but still permits significant solar heat gain unless paired with external shading (louvers or deep eaves).
Single Tinted/Bronze 4mm Acceptable Reduces glare and solar heat gain effectively for low-budget implementations, but absorbs heat into the glass pane which can radiate inwards if unventilated.
Single Clear 4mm Glass Poor Creates severe greenhouse conditions during the hot dry season. Avoid using on any unshaded East, West, or North facades.

5. Door Systems & Air Tightness

Doors must prevent hot air infiltration during summer and preserve internal temperatures during cool winter nights.

  • Steel Skin with PU Foam Core: The most energy-efficient entry door configuration. The steel skin provides structural stability and security, while the highly dense Polyurethane (PU) Foam core serves as an exceptional thermal break.
  • Hardwood (Pine): A natural carbon-sequestering option. Pine offers good natural thermal resistance but must be well-seasoned and sealed to prevent warping under Zambia’s contrasting wet and dry humidity extremes.

Strategic Summary for Zambia: To achieve net-zero or highly sustainable buildings in Lusaka, Ndola, or Livingstone, designers should prioritize a High Mass - External Insulation approach. Use Red Clay Face Bricks with Glass Wool insulation cavities, install Concrete Roof Tiles over insulated attics, lay down Polished Concrete or Granite floors, and specify Double Glazing Low-E windows on all sun-exposed facades.
